An incentive trip is a thank-you, not a work event. The trip itself is the message: "you made the year, and this is for you". That changes how you plan it: less agenda, more experiences, more surprise and more comfort.

## What makes an incentive trip memorable

- **Experiences money can't easily buy alone:** a private boat, a desert camp, a chef's table.
- **Surprise:** a reveal of the destination, a gift in the room, an unexpected evening.
- **Comfort:** good flights, smooth transfers, a beautiful hotel, nothing to organise.
- **Recognition:** a moment where leadership thanks people personally.

## Experience ideas

- **Private sailing or catamaran day** with lunch on board.
- **Hot-air balloon flight** at sunrise.
- **Chef's table dinner** or a private cooking class.
- **Desert night** with music and stargazing.
- **Spa afternoon.**
- **Wine tasting in a vineyard** in the Douro or Tuscany.
- **Exclusive access** to a museum or monument after hours.

## How to structure the trip

- **3 to 5 nights** is the norm.
- **Little or no work:** perhaps one short session with leadership.
- **One big experience per day**, with free time around it.
- **A gala or celebration dinner** on the last night.
- **Partners invited?** Many companies include partners, which makes the reward even stronger.

## FAQ

### What is an incentive trip?
A reward trip for employees who reached their targets, focused on experiences and recognition rather than work.

### How long should an incentive trip be?
Three to five nights is typical, enough for several memorable experiences without too much time away.

### Should partners be invited on incentive trips?
Many companies invite partners, as it makes the reward more meaningful and recognises the support of families.
