The venue is booked, the programme is ready, and then someone asks: "So, how is everyone getting there?" For distributed teams, flights are the hardest part of a retreat and the part most agencies leave to you.

Here is how to do it properly.

## Why "everyone books their own" doesn't work

It sounds simple, but it creates five problems:

1. **Arrivals spread over a whole day.** The opening session starts with half the team missing.
2. **Unequal prices.** The person who books late pays three times more.
3. **Expense chaos.** Forty receipts in different currencies to reconcile.
4. **No overview.** Nobody knows who is on which flight when there is a strike or a delay.
5. **Lost time.** Each person spends an hour comparing options.

## The central booking approach

When one team books every flight, you can:

- **Coordinate arrivals** within the same two- to three-hour window, so transfers can be grouped.
- **Use group fares** where they make sense, and individual fares where they are cheaper.
- **Keep one budget**, with one invoice.
- **Handle changes in one place**: cancellations, delays, name changes.

This is exactly what we do. We book [every participant's flight, from their own city](#comment/vols), as part of the retreat.

## Step by step

### 1. Map departure cities first
Before choosing a destination, list where everyone flies from. A destination with direct flights from all your hubs saves money and stress. [Lisbon](#retraite-lisbonne), [Barcelona](#retraite-barcelone) and [Athens](#retraite-athenes) are well connected from most of Europe.

### 2. Set an arrival window
Pick a window, for example 11:00 to 14:00 on day one, and a departure window on the last day. Work backwards from it for every city.

### 3. Book 8 to 12 weeks ahead
Fares for groups rise fast in the final weeks. Booking early also gives you the best choice of times.

### 4. Decide on fare types
Flexible fares cost more but protect you if numbers change. A mix often works best: flexible for people whose attendance is uncertain, standard for the rest.

### 5. Collect the right data
Full names exactly as in passports, dates of birth, nationality for non-Schengen trips, frequent flyer numbers and baggage needs.

![A sunlit airport terminal](bl05b)

### 6. Plan the transfers
Group arrivals by time and send one coach or minibus per wave. Nobody should be waiting two hours at the airport.

### 7. Have a plan for disruption
Someone must be reachable during travel days to rebook missed connections and update the venue. This is where a human answering the phone makes all the difference.

## How much should you budget?

For flights within Europe, expect roughly €150 to €450 per person return, depending on the routes and how early you book. See our full [retreat cost breakdown](#post-company-retreat-cost-per-person-europe) or use the [budget calculator](#budget), which includes flights.

## What about visas?

Most European destinations are inside the Schengen area. For [Marrakech](#retraite-marrakech), [Istanbul](#retraite-istanbul) or [Tbilisi](#retraite-georgie), check entry rules for every nationality in your team early.

## FAQ

### Can you book flights for a team spread across different countries?
Yes. We book each participant from their own city and coordinate arrivals so the group lands within the same window.

### Is it cheaper to book group flights or individual tickets?
It depends on the route. Group fares help on popular routes and peak dates; individual fares can be cheaper elsewhere. Central booking lets you choose the best option for each person.

### What happens if a flight is cancelled during the retreat?
When we book the flights, we rebook affected participants and update transfers and the venue, so nobody has to sort it out alone.
