Company Offsite vs Retreat vs Seminar: What's the Difference?

Offsite, retreat, seminar, kickoff, incentive: what each term really means, when to use which format, and how to choose for your team.

By Christopher Kwo · 21 November 2026 · 3 min read

"Offsite", "retreat", "seminar", "kickoff": people use these words interchangeably, and that creates confusion when you brief a venue, a manager or an agency. The words matter less than the format behind them, but knowing the differences helps you ask for the right thing.

Company offsite

An offsite is any work meeting that happens away from the office. It can be a single day in a hotel near the city or three days abroad. The focus is usually work: planning, strategy, workshops. Social time is there, but it supports the agenda.

Best for: quarterly or annual planning, cross-team alignment, reorganisations. See our company offsite format.

Company retreat

A retreat is an offsite with more emphasis on stepping back: fewer slides, more conversation, more nature and shared experiences. Retreats are almost always overnight, often in a quieter place: a quinta in the Douro, a villa in Mallorca, a mountain lodge in Zakopane.

Best for: connection, culture, remote teams meeting in person, leadership reflection. A team-building retreat is a retreat where shared activities are the core of the programme.

Seminar

In French-speaking countries, séminaire is the generic word for any company offsite. In English, a seminar usually means a more structured, educational session: talks, training, presentations. If you are writing to an English-speaking venue, "offsite" or "retreat" is clearer.

Leadership retreat

A small-group retreat (6 to 25 people) for founders, executives or managers. The programme is intense and confidential: strategy, hard decisions, team dynamics. Venue privacy and a calm meeting room matter more than activities. Read our leadership retreat agenda or see the leadership retreat format.

Sales kickoff (SKO)

A high-energy event, usually at the start of the year, to launch targets, celebrate wins and train the sales team. Kickoffs are often larger (30 to 500 people), with a plenary stage, breakouts and a big evening. See our sales kickoff ideas and the kickoff format.

Incentive trip

A reward trip for top performers, sometimes with partners. There is little or no work: the trip itself is the message. Experiences, comfort and surprise are what count. See incentive trip ideas or our incentive format.

Client and partner event

An event where you host clients, partners or distributors: a launch, a conference with a social programme, a hospitality weekend. The quality of every detail reflects on your brand. See our client and partner events.

Which one do you need?

Ask yourself what people should feel and do on the Monday after. If the answer is "know exactly what we're doing next year", you need an offsite. If it's "trust each other more", you need a retreat. If it's "feel rewarded", you need an incentive.

Frequently asked questions

Is an offsite the same as a retreat?

Not quite. An offsite is any meeting away from the office and is usually work-focused. A retreat is overnight and gives more space to connection, nature and shared experiences.

What is a corporate seminar?

In French, "séminaire" means any company offsite. In English, a seminar usually refers to a structured training or educational session.

How many days should an offsite last?

Most offsites last one to three days. Retreats work best over two to three nights so people have time to disconnect.
